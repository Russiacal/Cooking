I had three pounds of bone-in pork loin chops and came back to maiale al latte, which I had made before with pork shoulder.

Chops cook much faster than a roast, so rather than braising everything together I seared them, took them out, and reduced the milk on its own before returning them. I dry-brined at 1% salt for about eight hours, seared in butter and olive oil in two batches in the oval Dutch oven, deglazed with white wine, and reduced four cups of milk uncovered down to about a cup and a quarter over roughly forty minutes. I used about twice the lemon peel and sage the instructions called for, and I kept the garlic in the whole time instead of pulling it after the initial fry, which softened it nicely. The recipe I made before kept everything in for the whole cooking time, which is why I did it this way — it seemed a shame to throw away the garlic after just a short cook.

The chops went back into the sauce for about fifteen minutes rather than six. At six they did not feel like they had even started to reheat. They came out super tender this way, which I love as they are always at risk of overcooking and coming out dry. The chops made it easier to pull a portion off for Milo early, but I preferred how everything worked together in the long slow shoulder braise.

## Ingredients

- 3 lbs bone-in pork loin chops (4 chops)
- Salt for dry brine, 1% by weight
- 4 cups whole milk
- 1/2 cup dry white wine
- Sage leaves, about double the called-for amount
- Lemon peel, about double the called-for amount
- 6 garlic cloves, smashed, left in for the whole braise
- Butter
- Olive oil
